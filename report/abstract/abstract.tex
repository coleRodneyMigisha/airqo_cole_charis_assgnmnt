\title{Forecasting Hourly PM\textsubscript{2.5} from Sparse Low-Cost Sensor Records in Uganda: Gap-Aware Imputation, Time-Aware Validation and Cloud Deployment}
\author{Charis Opol\authorrefmark{1}\,\orcidicon{https://orcid.org/0009-0003-7417-7984}, Cole Rodney Migisha\authorrefmark{1}\,\orcidicon{https://orcid.org/0009-0000-5311-2485}}
\address[1]{Uganda Christian University, Mukono, Uganda, Faculty of Engineering, Design and Technology, Department of Computing and Technology  \\b29654@students.ucu.ac.ug \\b29657@students.ucu.ac.ug}

\begin{abstract}
Low-cost sensor networks such as AirQo have expanded PM\textsubscript{2.5} monitoring in Ugandan cities, but intermittent device availability leaves short, fragmented records, and it is unclear how well such records support forecasting. We develop and deploy a pipeline that predicts hourly PM\textsubscript{2.5} from 14{,}600 records of 149 AirQo sensors, which contain readings on only eight days in three bursts. PM\textsubscript{10} was excluded as an input because it is a same-hour co-measurement of the same particles rather than a driver of PM\textsubscript{2.5}, and is unavailable in advance. Extreme readings were replaced by the nearest normal value from the same sensor, and weather gaps were filled by windowed interpolation, time-of-day profiles or a BRITS-style bidirectional LSTM. Twelve models were compared under chronological hold-out, expanding-window and leave-sensors-out validation; SMOTE was tested and rejected. From weather, time and sensor identity alone, a tuned support vector regressor achieved a hold-out MAE of 9.34~\(\mu\)g/m\textsuperscript{3} (\(R^2 = 0.34\)), against 17.60~\(\mu\)g/m\textsuperscript{3} for a mean baseline; adding the previous three hours of PM\textsubscript{2.5} reduced the MAE to 8.43~\(\mu\)g/m\textsuperscript{3} (LightGBM, \(R^2 = 0.42\)). The bidirectional imputer reduced imputation error by 21\% but changed prediction error by only \(-0.4\)\%. On a later full-month dataset (August 2026), the history-based models retained skill (MAE 13.70 against 19.21~\(\mu\)g/m\textsuperscript{3}), whereas the weather-only models performed close to the baseline, consistent with drift in six of nine input features. The forecast model is deployed as a cloud API with a dashboard. In sparse networks, recent pollutant history matters more than weather or sophisticated imputation.
\end{abstract}
